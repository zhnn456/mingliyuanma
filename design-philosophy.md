# Ink & Vermilion — 墨朱美学

## Design Philosophy

**墨朱** is an aesthetic movement born from the intersection of Chinese scholarly tradition and cosmic geometry. It draws from the visual language of ancient Chinese scrolls (卷轴), court astrology charts (钦天监), and the quiet precision of Song-dynasty woodblock printing. Every element exists not as decoration but as a carrier of meaning — the vermilion seal (朱印) is not a stamp but a signature of authority; the ink line (墨线) is not a border but a demarcation of cosmic order; the rice-paper texture (宣纸纹) is not a backdrop but the very fabric upon which destiny is inscribed.

Space in 墨朱 is sacred and hierarchical. The twelve palaces (十二宫) are positioned in a 4×4 mandala grid, each cell a self-contained world, yet all bound by invisible threads of cosmic relation. The center — the 中宫 — is the axis mundi, the still point around which all destiny revolves. Negative space is not emptiness but breath (气): the deliberate void between palaces allows the eye to rest and the mind to contemplate. This is meticulous spatial crafting, the product of deep expertise in both Chinese aesthetic principles and information design.

Color operates as a semantic system, not decoration. Vermilion (朱砂红 #9B2C2C) signals authority, transformation, and the active principle — it marks the 命宫 (life palace), the 四化 (four transformations), and critical boundaries. Gold (金箔 #C9A962) marks the celestial — 主星 (major stars), the 命主/身主 (soul/body masters), and moments of cosmic significance. Ink-black (墨 #1A1A2E) anchors the structure — star names, palace boundaries, and the quiet scaffolding of the chart. Rice-paper cream (宣纸 #F5E6C8) is the ground from which all meaning emerges. Every color choice has been calibrated through countless hours of refinement, ensuring the palette feels as though it was drawn from an ancient manuscript rather than a modern design tool.

Typography is a vehicle of presence. Song-dynasty typefaces (宋体) carry the weight of authority — they are the script of official records and cosmic documentation. Small, precise sans-serif labels (细体) serve as whispered annotations, never competing with the main text. The hierarchy is absolute: palace names are rendered in bold 宋体 at 14-16px, star names at 12px with color-coded modifiers, and metadata at 9-10px in light sans. This typographic system is the product of painstaking attention to every stroke weight and spacing relationship, ensuring readability at every scale.

Composition follows the principles of 观 (contemplation) and 序 (sequence). The eye enters through the central 中宫, travels outward along the 4×4 grid, then descends into the textual analysis below. Each view mode — 飞星 (flying stars), 三合 (three-combination), 四化 (four transformations) — reorients the visual hierarchy without disrupting the underlying grid. SVG overlays (curves for flying stars, straight lines for three-combination) are rendered with the precision of celestial instruments, their colors and weights encoding meaning. The result is a system where every pixel serves a purpose, every line tells a story, and the whole feels like a artifact from a master calligrapher's studio — meticulously crafted, labored over with care, and worthy of sustained contemplation.

The final work must appear as though it took countless hours to create, was labored over with painstaking attention, and comes from someone at the absolute top of their field in both Chinese aesthetic tradition and modern interface design. It should feel like a document from the Imperial Observatory — authoritative, precise, and quietly powerful.
